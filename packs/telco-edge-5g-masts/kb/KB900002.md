# Radio Unit Overtemperature: TX Power Derating on Ericsson AIR 3268 AAS Radio

| | |
|---|---|
| **Article ID** | KB900002 |
| **Version** | 3 |
| **Last updated** | 2026-01-05 |
| **Classification** | Break/Fix — Radio Unit / Thermal Management |
| **Default severity** | Warning escalating to Critical (Sev 2 — capacity degraded, radio remains in service under derating) |
| **Audience** | RAN field engineers, network operations center (NOC) |
| **Applies to** | Ericsson AIR 3268 Antenna Integrated Radio (AAS massive-MIMO), autonomous fan-based cooling |

## Summary

The AIR 3268's fan unit operates autonomously, adjusting speed against ambient temperature to keep the radio core within its rated operating range. When cooling airflow falls below what the current thermal load requires — most often a fan degrading rather than failing outright — radio core temperature climbs. As it approaches the unit's thermal limit, the radio automatically applies TX power derating: reducing transmit power to cut the heat the power amplifiers generate, trading cell capacity and edge coverage for continued safe operation rather than an uncontrolled shutdown. This is a protective mechanism working as designed, not itself the fault — the fault is whatever degraded the cooling in the first place. If derating successfully holds temperature within limits, the radio stays in service at reduced capacity; if the underlying cooling issue is severe enough that even full derating can't hold the line, the radio unit shuts down entirely to prevent component damage, which is a complete capacity loss at the site rather than the partial, degraded-but-in-service state this article's scenario describes.

## Affected products

- Ericsson AIR 3268 Antenna Integrated Radio (AAS massive-MIMO)
- Applicable to the broader AIR family (3200/6000 series) sharing the same autonomous fan-based thermal management design

## Error signature

The canonical signatures for this fault are:

- `Radio unit overtemperature`
- `temperature exceptionally high`
- `TX power derating`

Radio unit thermal management log:

```
CRIT  radio[gnb-site-01]: Radio unit overtemperature — temperature exceptionally high, TX power derating applied
CRIT  radio[gnb-site-01]: Radio core temp 71C — TX power reduced to 60% of rated output
```

## Symptoms

- Fan speed reported below what's expected for the current ambient/thermal load — the earliest actionable signal, often visible well before temperature crosses a warning threshold
- Radio core temperature trends upward over tens of minutes rather than spiking instantly, consistent with a gradually degrading cooling capacity rather than a sudden hardware failure
- Automatic TX power derating applied once temperature crosses the unit's threshold (commonly around 70C for this radio class)
- Reduced TX power correlates with reduced cell edge coverage/throughput — most users unaffected, cell-edge and high-load users see the impact first
- Temperature typically stabilizes (not necessarily drops significantly) once derating is active — the derating is designed to hold the line, not fully resolve the thermal issue, which is why the fan fault still needs physical remediation

## Sample log excerpt

Representative sequence from `gnb-site-01` (Downtown Rooftop, AIR 3268):

```
13:00:00Z INFO  radio[gnb-site-01]: Radio core temp 42C, ambient 28C, fan speed nominal (auto)
14:22:18Z WARN  radio[gnb-site-01]: Radio unit fan speed below expected for current ambient — cooling airflow degraded
14:22:18Z WARN  radio[gnb-site-01]: Fan speed 1400 RPM (expected ~2600 RPM at current thermal load)
14:31:42Z WARN  radio[gnb-site-01]: Radio core temp 58C — trending up, fan speed still below expected
14:47:33Z CRIT  radio[gnb-site-01]: Radio unit overtemperature — temperature exceptionally high, TX power derating applied
14:47:33Z CRIT  radio[gnb-site-01]: Radio core temp 71C — TX power reduced to 60% of rated output
15:05:02Z INFO  radio[gnb-site-01]: Radio core temp 68C — stable under derated TX power, fan speed unchanged at 1400 RPM
```

The fan speed reading (1400 RPM against an expected ~2600 RPM) staying flat even after derating kicks in is the key diagnostic detail — it confirms the fan itself hasn't recovered and the temperature stability seen is purely a result of the TX power reduction, not any improvement in cooling.

## Impact

- **Workload impact:** Reduced TX power means reduced cell edge coverage and capacity at this site while derating is active. Most users in good coverage see no effect; users at cell edge or during high-load periods may experience degraded throughput or get handed over to neighboring sites.
- **Service risk:** Medium. Sustained overtemperature operation, even successfully held by derating, accelerates component aging in the power amplifiers and other radio electronics. If the underlying cooling fault worsens to the point derating can no longer hold temperature within limits, the radio shuts down entirely — a full capacity loss at the site, not just a degraded one.
- **Estimated remediation duration:** ~10 minutes to confirm derating status and fan alarm state. Field technician cooling-system service (fan replacement, cooling element cleaning) is typically scheduled within 1-3 business days depending on severity and site access.

## Root cause

In observed order of frequency, per Ericsson thermal management guidance:

1. **Fan bearing wear or motor degradation** — the fan continues running but below its rated speed for the given thermal load, exactly as seen in this event; a common wear-driven failure mode rather than a sudden fan stop.
2. **Cooling element/heatsink fouling** — dust, pollen, or debris accumulation on cooling fins restricts heat dissipation even with a fully healthy fan; Ericsson troubleshooting guidance explicitly calls out purging cooling air openings as a standard first check.
3. **Ambient temperature exceeding site design assumptions** — sustained heat wave conditions or a site installed without adequate margin above the unit's rated ambient (commonly 30C max for indoor-adjacent mounting) pushing normal operation into the derating threshold even with healthy cooling hardware.
4. **Radio installation obstruction** — units mounted with insufficient clearance, or with fan units improperly removed/blocked (Ericsson explicitly warns against installing radios with removed fan units stacked above each other), restricting airflow independent of the fan's own health.

## Remediation steps

### Step 1 — Confirm automatic TX derating profile is holding temperature within safe limits (`apply_tx_derating_profile`)

This is largely a verification step, since derating engages automatically — confirm it's working as intended rather than applying it manually:

```
OSS console: confirm radio unit shows active TX derating and current TX power percentage
Confirm radio core temperature trend has flattened or is decreasing since derating engaged
If temperature continues climbing despite derating, this is a more urgent condition — proceed to Step 3 immediately with elevated priority
```

### Step 2 — Verify fan unit alarm status and airflow sensor readings (`verify_fan_alarms`)

```
OSS console: check fan unit RPM against expected value for current ambient/thermal load
Confirm whether a fan-specific alarm has been raised independent of the overtemperature alarm
Note fan RPM reading for the field technician dispatch in Step 3 — this narrows the on-site diagnosis (fan replacement vs. cooling-element cleaning vs. ambient/installation issue)
```

### Step 3 — Schedule field technician site visit for fan/cooling inspection (`schedule_site_visit`)

```
Dispatch with the fan RPM deficit and temperature trend from Steps 1-2 to focus the inspection
On-site checklist: fan physical inspection, cooling element/heatsink fouling check, installation clearance verification
Replace fan unit or clean cooling elements as findings indicate; do not close the ticket on a visual inspection alone if fan RPM remains below expected
```

### Step 4 — Monitor radio temperature trend until confirmed stable (`monitor_temp_trend`)

```
After field service, confirm fan RPM returns to expected range for current ambient
Confirm radio core temperature trends down and TX derating automatically clears once temperature returns below the threshold with margin
Monitor for at least one full diurnal cycle (covering peak ambient temperature) to confirm the fix holds under worst-case daily conditions, not just at the time of repair
```

## Escalation

- Temperature continues climbing despite active TX derating → escalate immediately to NOC as an imminent radio-shutdown risk; do not wait for the scheduled Step 3 site visit, expedite dispatch.
- Fan RPM remains below expected after a fan replacement in Step 3 → escalate to Ericsson field service for radio unit diagnosis; may indicate a control/sensor fault rather than a mechanical fan issue.
- Multiple radios at the same site or nearby sites show correlated overtemperature events during the same period → escalate to network operations for a heat-wave/ambient-condition review rather than treating as independent hardware faults; may warrant a temporary site-wide capacity/derating policy review.
