# GNSS Reference Lost: Time Sync Failure and Holdover on Baseband 6630

| | |
|---|---|
| **Article ID** | KB900001 |
| **Version** | 4 |
| **Last updated** | 2025-11-20 |
| **Classification** | Critical — Timing / Synchronization Subsystem |
| **Default severity** | Critical (Sev 1 — holdover active, site-wide outage on expiry) |
| **Audience** | RAN field engineers, network operations center (NOC), transport/sync engineering |
| **Applies to** | Baseband 6630 with AIR 3268 AAS radios, GNSS-referenced PRTC-A timing, TDD 5G NR |

## Summary

TDD (Time Division Duplex) 5G requires all cells in a coverage area to be tightly time-synchronized — a phase error budget on the order of ±1.5 microseconds — so that uplink and downlink slot timing aligns across neighboring cells and doesn't cause co-channel interference. GNSS (typically GPS/GLONASS/Galileo) is the primary traceable time reference for this, per PRTC-A grade. When the GNSS receiver loses lock — insufficient tracked satellites for a valid fix — the baseband automatically falls back to holdover, continuing to derive timing from its local Stratum 3E oscillator alone. Holdover is a bounded-time safety margin, not a permanent fallback: the oscillator drifts slowly from the last known-good GNSS timestamp, and once drift exceeds the TDD sync budget, the site's cells shut down automatically to prevent transmitting out of alignment and interfering with neighboring sites. The system displays a holdover countdown specifically so operations can act before that shutdown, not after.

## Affected products

- Baseband 6630, AIR 3268 Antenna Integrated Radio (AAS massive-MIMO)
- GNSS-referenced PRTC-A timing sources
- TDD 5G NR deployments (this failure mode does not apply the same way to FDD sites, which have looser sync requirements)

## Error signature

The canonical signatures for this fault are:

- `GNSS reference lost`
- `time sync failure`
- `holdover`

Baseband sync subsystem log:

```
CRIT  sync[gnb-site-03]: GNSS reference lost — time sync failure, cells entering holdover on Stratum 3E oscillator
CRIT  sync[gnb-site-03]: Holdover initiated — estimated phase drift budget 4h 12m before TDD sync-loss threshold
```

## Symptoms

- GNSS satellite tracking count drops below the minimum required for a valid fix (commonly 6 for a stable 3D fix) — a gradual decline over minutes is typical, not usually instantaneous
- GNSS antenna signal strength reported as degraded, pointing at an antenna, cable, or obstruction issue rather than a receiver hardware fault
- Baseband declares GNSS reference lost and initiates holdover on the Stratum 3E oscillator
- A holdover countdown is raised at the OSS/NOC showing estimated time remaining before the TDD sync-loss threshold is reached
- Cells remain in service throughout holdover — this is not an immediate outage, which is exactly why the countdown window matters for remediation prioritization
- If a secondary PTP grandmaster or SyncE reference is provisioned at the site, it is typically available but not automatically selected as the active source without an operator or automated policy action

## Sample log excerpt

Representative sequence from `gnb-site-03` (Riverside Water Tank):

```
04:10:00Z INFO  sync[gnb-site-03]: GNSS receiver locked — 9 satellites tracked, PRTC-A traceable
05:32:14Z WARN  sync[gnb-site-03]: GNSS satellite tracking count dropped — 6 satellites tracked
05:41:07Z WARN  sync[gnb-site-03]: GNSS satellite tracking count dropped — 4 satellites tracked, below minimum for fix (6)
05:41:07Z WARN  sync[gnb-site-03]: GNSS antenna signal strength degraded — possible antenna/cable fault or obstruction
05:43:29Z CRIT  sync[gnb-site-03]: GNSS reference lost — time sync failure, cells entering holdover on Stratum 3E oscillator
05:43:29Z CRIT  sync[gnb-site-03]: Holdover initiated — estimated phase drift budget 4h 12m before TDD sync-loss threshold
05:43:30Z WARN  sync[gnb-site-03]: PTP grandmaster (secondary) — available, not yet selected as active source
05:44:02Z INFO  oss[central]: Holdover countdown displayed — 4h 08m remaining to estimated sync-loss shutdown
```

The tracked-satellite count falling from 9 to 4 over roughly 9 minutes, with the antenna signal-strength warning logged at the same moment as the below-minimum reading, is the pattern that distinguishes an antenna/cabling fault from a brief atmospheric or constellation-geometry dip that would typically self-resolve without dropping this far.

## Impact

- **Workload impact:** No immediate service impact if a backup sync source is engaged before the holdover window expires. If holdover expires first, all TDD cells at this site drop simultaneously, taking the site fully out of service until GNSS or another valid reference is restored.
- **Service risk:** High. TDD 5G's tight inter-cell timing requirement means a site continuing to transmit on a drifted, out-of-budget clock (rather than shutting down cleanly) would risk degrading neighboring sites' performance too — this is why the shutdown-on-expiry behavior exists, and why proactive holdover-margin remediation is preferable to waiting for that safety mechanism to trigger.
- **Estimated remediation duration:** ~15 minutes to switch to a backup sync source, if provisioned. GNSS antenna physical fault diagnosis and repair is a field dispatch task, typically same-day to next-day depending on site access (rooftop/tower access constraints vary considerably by site type).

## Root cause

In observed order of frequency:

1. **GNSS antenna or cable fault** — corrosion, water ingress, or a damaged connector at the antenna or feed cable degrades received signal strength below the threshold needed to maintain lock, consistent with the gradual satellite-count decline and antenna signal-strength warning in this event.
2. **Physical obstruction of the antenna's sky view** — new construction, vegetation growth, or ice/snow accumulation blocking line-of-sight to enough satellites for a fix; more common at sites with limited original sky-view margin.
3. **GNSS jamming or interference** — intentional or unintentional RF interference in the GNSS band; distinguishable when multiple nearby sites lose GNSS lock simultaneously, which points away from a single site's hardware and toward an area-wide interference source.
4. **GNSS receiver module hardware fault** — less common; suspected when antenna and cabling both inspect clean and the receiver still fails to achieve lock even under open-sky conditions.

## Remediation steps

### Step 1 — Switch baseband to backup PTP grandmaster / SyncE reference (`switch_backup_sync_source`)

Time-critical — do this before investigating the physical GNSS fault, since it extends the site's safety margin immediately:

```
Baseband OSS console: Sync Source → select secondary PTP grandmaster (or SyncE, if that's the provisioned backup)
Confirm sync source transition completes and phase error returns within the TDD budget (+-1.5us)
Confirm holdover countdown clears once a valid external reference is re-established
```

If no backup sync source is provisioned at this site, proceed directly to Step 2 with elevated urgency given the holdover countdown continues regardless.

### Step 2 — Verify remaining holdover margin against oscillator drift budget (`verify_holdover_margin`)

```
Check current holdover countdown at OSS — confirm remaining time against the site's rated Stratum 3E drift budget
If Step 1 succeeded, this step confirms the countdown has cleared
If Step 1 was not possible (no backup source), use this margin to set the urgency of Step 3's dispatch
```

### Step 3 — Dispatch field technician to inspect GNSS antenna and cabling (`dispatch_gnss_antenna_inspection`)

```
Dispatch with explicit inspection scope: antenna physical condition, connector/cable integrity, sky-view obstruction check
Bring a spectrum analyzer if area-wide interference is suspected (multiple sites affected) rather than assuming a single-site antenna fault
Replace antenna, cable, or connector as needed; re-verify satellite tracking count returns to normal (typically 8+ satellites in open sky) after repair
```

### Step 4 — Confirm all TDD cells remain in service after sync source recovery (`confirm_cells_in_service`)

```
Verify all cells at the site show normal operational status post-recovery, whether recovery came via Step 1 (backup source) or Step 3 (GNSS repair)
Confirm phase error has returned within the TDD budget, not just that a source is "selected"
Monitor for at least one full holdover-cycle-equivalent period to confirm the fix is stable, not a transient reacquisition
```

## Escalation

- No backup PTP/SyncE source is provisioned at the site and holdover margin drops below 30 minutes remaining before Step 3 dispatch can complete → escalate immediately to NOC leadership as an imminent site-outage event; this may warrant emergency temporary sync equipment dispatch rather than routine field service scheduling.
- Multiple sites in the same geographic area lose GNSS lock within a short window of each other → escalate to RF/spectrum engineering for jamming/interference investigation rather than treating as independent single-site antenna faults.
- GNSS antenna and cabling inspect clean in Step 3 but the receiver still fails to achieve lock under open-sky conditions → escalate to the radio manufacturer's field service for baseband GNSS receiver module diagnosis.
