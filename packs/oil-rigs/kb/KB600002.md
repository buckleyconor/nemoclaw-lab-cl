# VFD IGBT Overtemperature Fault 42F1 on NOV TDS-11SA Top Drive

| | |
|---|---|
| **Article ID** | KB600002 |
| **Version** | 4 |
| **Last updated** | 2025-09-27 |
| **Classification** | Break/Fix — VFD / Electrical Drive System |
| **Default severity** | Critical (Sev 2 — auto-derate active, drive has not tripped) |
| **Audience** | Rig electricians, top drive technicians, driller |
| **Applies to** | NOV TDS-11SA/TDS-11SAE Top Drive with ABB ACS880 VFD, 400HP AC drilling motors |

## Summary

Fault 42F1 (IGBT Overtemperature, also logged historically as 4210 on earlier ACS880 firmware) fires when the drive's estimated IGBT junction temperature exceeds its rated threshold. The ACS880 monitors heatsink, control-board, and ambient intake temperatures continuously and applies an automatic torque derate before a hard trip if the estimate keeps climbing. On the TDS-11SA, each of the two 400HP AC drilling motors is force-air cooled by a dedicated 4HP TEFC blower drawing intake air through the brake cover, so the fault almost always traces back to a cooling-airflow deficit rather than the IGBT itself — the semiconductor is doing exactly what it's rated for at a heatsink temperature the airflow failed to hold down. Left unaddressed, a fault that starts as a partial derate can progress to a full drive trip, which mid-connection is an uncommanded stop of rotation with pipe in the slips — plan the response to catch this at the derate stage.

## Affected products

- NOV TDS-11SA, TDS-11SAE, TDS-11-800/1400 Top Drive Systems
- ABB ACS880 industrial drive (IGBT supply control firmware)
- 400HP forced-air-cooled AC drilling motors (two per unit, 800HP total)

## Error signature

The canonical signatures for this fault are:

- `VFD IGBT overtemperature`
- `drive overtemperature fault`
- `top drive derate`

Top drive VFD controller:

```
CRIT  vfd[top-drive-01]: VFD IGBT overtemperature fault 42F1 — top drive AC drilling motor 1, estimated junction temp 94C
WARN  vfd[top-drive-01]: Drive overtemperature fault — cooling blower airflow below rated, top drive derate imminent
```

## Symptoms

- IGBT heatsink temperature trends upward over 15-30 minutes at otherwise steady torque/RPM — not a sudden spike, which distinguishes this from a short-circuit-triggered IGBT overload fault
- Cooling blower airflow sensor reads below nominal (rated ~3600 RPM, 4HP TEFC) before the temperature threshold is crossed
- Automatic torque derate (commonly to 40%) applied to the affected motor before a full trip
- Driller reports reduced rate of penetration / torque limit alarm on the console
- The unaffected drilling motor (units have two) typically shows normal IGBT temperature, confirming the fault is airflow-localized rather than an ambient/environmental condition affecting the whole VFD cabinet

## Sample log excerpt

Representative sequence from `top-drive-01` (TDS-11SA, drive motor 1) during active drilling:

```
09:14:00Z INFO  vfd[top-drive-01]: Drive motor 1 running — 145 RPM, 62% torque, IGBT temp 58C
10:03:47Z WARN  vfd[top-drive-01]: IGBT heatsink temp trending up — 61C to 78C over 22 min at steady torque
10:03:47Z WARN  vfd[top-drive-01]: Cooling blower 1 airflow sensor reading below rated — 62% of nominal
10:07:02Z WARN  vfd[top-drive-01]: IGBT heatsink temp 85C — approaching overtemperature threshold (90C)
10:07:02Z WARN  vfd[top-drive-01]: Drive overtemperature fault — cooling blower airflow below rated, top drive derate imminent
10:08:41Z CRIT  vfd[top-drive-01]: VFD IGBT overtemperature fault 42F1 — top drive AC drilling motor 1, estimated junction temp 94C
10:08:41Z CRIT  vfd[top-drive-01]: Fault code 42F1 latched — automatic torque derate to 40% applied
10:08:42Z INFO  vfd[top-drive-01]: Drive motor 2 unaffected — IGBT temp 63C, no fault
```

The blower airflow reading dropping to 62% of nominal 40 minutes before the fault latches is the earliest actionable signal — catching it there avoids the derate entirely on a future occurrence.

## Impact

- **Workload impact:** Reduced rate of penetration while torque is limited (typically 10-20 minutes to resolve). An uncommanded full drive trip during an active connection would be substantially more disruptive — this remediation is explicitly aimed at preventing that escalation.
- **Service risk:** Medium — repeated overtemperature events accelerate IGBT thermal-cycling fatigue, shortening drive component life. A blocked cooling blower left unaddressed risks a full trip during a critical operation such as tripping pipe in or out of hole.
- **Estimated remediation duration:** ~20 minutes to reduce load, verify cooling, and reset the fault; blower motor or duct service (if the airflow deficit doesn't clear) is a longer maintenance task outside this window.

## Root cause

In observed order of frequency, per ABB ACS880 troubleshooting guidance and rig-floor experience:

1. **Cooling blower degradation** — bearing wear or partial motor winding fault on the 4HP TEFC blower reduces airflow below the rated volume without stopping the blower outright, so it still shows "running" while underperforming.
2. **Intake duct or filter restriction** — dust, cuttings, or debris accumulation in the brake-cover air intake or rigid ducting to the motor restricts airflow even with a healthy blower.
3. **Excessive ambient temperature** — VFD cabinet or motor compartment ambient above rated spec (commonly 40-50C depending on model), especially in enclosed sub-structure spaces with inadequate ventilation.
4. **Motor power vs. drive power mismatch** — sustained torque demand exceeding the drive's continuous rating for the ambient conditions present, which is a duty-cycle/loading issue rather than a hardware fault.

## Remediation steps

### Step 1 — Reduce top drive RPM/torque setpoint (`reduce_torque_setpoint`)

Cut thermal load on the affected drive immediately, before the fault progresses from derate to trip:

```
Driller console: reduce top drive torque setpoint to 50% of pre-fault value
Confirm drive motor 1 IGBT temp trend flattens or begins to fall
```

### Step 2 — Verify cooling blower airflow and ducting (`verify_cooling_blower`)

```
Physically inspect blower 1 — confirm rotation, listen for bearing noise
Check intake at brake cover / air intake for debris or cuttings buildup
Inspect rigid ducting run to drive motor 1 for kinks, blockage, or disconnection
Compare airflow sensor reading against blower 2 (drive motor 2) as a healthy baseline
```

### Step 3 — Reset VFD fault (`reset_drive_fault`)

Only after IGBT temperature has fallen back below the fault threshold (typically requires 10-15 minutes with load reduced and airflow restored):

```
ABB ACS880 control panel: confirm heatsink temp < 75C before reset
Fault reset via drive panel or driller console fault-reset command
Ramp torque back up incrementally, watching IGBT temp trend at each step
```

Resetting before the temperature has genuinely recovered will re-latch the fault within minutes and doesn't diagnose anything new.

### Step 4 — Monitor IGBT temperature trend (`monitor_igbt_temp`)

```
Trend IGBT temp on both drive motors through the next full connection cycle
Flag for maintenance if temp approaches 80C again at normal torque before the next crew change
```

Confirm stability through at least one full connection before resuming unrestricted torque — a fault that clears on reset but recurs within the same tour indicates the airflow deficit wasn't actually resolved in Step 2.

## Escalation

- Fault recurs within the same tour after blower/duct inspection finds no obvious cause → escalate to rig electrician for blower motor winding test and drive cabinet ambient survey.
- IGBT temperature fails to recover below threshold after 20+ minutes at reduced torque → do not force a reset; escalate to ABB field service, possible IGBT module degradation rather than a cooling issue.
- Fault occurs on both drive motors simultaneously → points to cabinet-level ambient or ventilation problem, not a single blower fault; escalate to rig maintenance supervisor for sub-structure ventilation review.
