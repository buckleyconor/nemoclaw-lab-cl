# PSU Redundancy Loss on PowerEdge XE9780L — Detection and Response

| | |
|---|---|
| **Article ID** | KB000789 |
| **Version** | 5 |
| **Last updated** | 2026-09-03 |
| **Classification** | Break/Fix — Power Infrastructure |
| **Default severity** | Critical (Sev 2 — node still serving) |
| **Audience** | Datacenter operations, facilities |
| **Applies to** | Dell PowerEdge XE9780L on a 33 kW powershelf (6× 5500 W AC PSUs) |

## Summary

The XE9780L does not carry its own chassis power supplies. It draws from a shared 33 kW powershelf of six hot-swappable 5500 W AC supplies, which feeds the sleds in its IR7000 rack power domain, with the six supplies split across two PDU branches (A/B feed). Loss of a single PSU — whether from a supply failure or loss of its AC input — does not interrupt service, because the surviving five still cover the connected load. What it removes is the reserve: under an N+1 redundancy policy the power budget is installed capacity minus one supply, so losing a PSU drops the budget a full 5500 W in one step. When the connected load then exceeds that reduced budget, the rack power manager engages GPU power capping to rebuild a single-fault margin, and training throughput drops until the supply is restored. Because the shelf is shared, a second loss takes down every sled in the domain, not just one node.

## Affected products

- Dell PowerEdge XE9780L with 8× NVIDIA HGX B300 SXM6 GPUs at 1100 W each
- 33 kW powershelf, 6× 5500 W AC PSUs (200–240 VAC or 240 VDC), N+1 redundancy policy
- IR7000 rack power domains serving more than one sled from a single shelf

## Error signature

The canonical signatures for this fault are:

- `PSU 2 input lost`
- `power supply failure`

iDRAC Lifecycle Log message IDs: `OEM.1.0.PSULost` (severity Critical), often preceded by `PSU.1.0.InputVoltageWarning`. The slot number in the message identifies the affected supply within the shelf.

## Symptoms

- iDRAC Lifecycle Log records `OEM.1.0.PSULost` with severity Critical
- The affected PSU's status LED on the powershelf is amber or unlit
- iDRAC Dashboard shows the PSU as `Failed`, `Absent`, or `Input Lost`
- If connected load exceeds the reduced N+1 budget, iDRAC logs `Power budget exceeded` and DCGM reports GPU power limits reduced (e.g. capped from 1100 W to 900 W), with a corresponding drop in training throughput
- Peer sleds on the same shelf report the event and the cap simultaneously — a useful confirmation that the fault is shelf-level, not node-level
- An input-voltage warning (`PSU.1.0.InputVoltageWarning`) in the minutes before the loss points at the facility feed rather than the PSU itself

## Sample log excerpt

Representative sequence from a production occurrence on `gpu-server-01` (03:11 local — note the voltage sag preceding the loss):

```
03:10:00Z INFO  iDRAC[LifecycleLog]: Powershelf healthy. PSU 1-6 online at 5500W each — 33000W installed. Redundancy policy: N+1.
03:10:30Z INFO  iDRAC[LifecycleLog]: GPU workload steady. Shelf draw: 22600W of 27500W budget (2 sleds connected)
03:11:45Z WARN  iDRAC[LifecycleLog]: PSU.1.0.InputVoltageWarning — PSU 2: input voltage fluctuation detected (199V, threshold 200V)
03:11:47Z CRIT  iDRAC[LifecycleLog]: PSU 2 input power lost — redundancy degraded
03:11:47Z CRIT  iDRAC[LifecycleLog]: OEM.1.0.PSULost — Power supply failure on PSU 2. Powershelf now operating on 5 of 6 PSUs.
03:11:48Z INFO  iDRAC[LifecycleLog]: Installed capacity 27500W. N+1 budget now 22000W. Current draw 22600W — OVER BUDGET
03:11:49Z CRIT  iDRAC[LifecycleLog]: Power budget exceeded. Initiating GPU power capping.
03:11:49Z WARN  dcgm[1234]: GPU power limits reduced: all GPUs capped at 900W (was 1100W)
```

In this incident the shelf still had 27500 W of installed capacity against a 22600 W draw, so nothing was starved — but the N+1 policy budgets against the loss of one more supply (4 × 5500 W = 22000 W), and 22600 W exceeds that. The cap is the rack power manager rebuilding the single-fault margin, expected protective behavior, not a second fault.

## Impact

- **Workload impact:** None immediately — every sled on the shelf keeps running and no drain or migration is required. If automatic GPU power capping engages, training throughput drops less than proportionally to the cap (an 1100 W → 900 W cap typically costs 10–15% throughput) until redundancy is restored.
- **Service risk:** Degraded power redundancy across the whole shelf until the PSU or its feed is restored. A second PSU loss during this window can force an unplanned shutdown of every sled in the power domain, hard-killing all their GPU workloads without checkpoint — the blast radius is the shelf, not the node.
- **Data risk:** None while the shelf holds.
- **Estimated remediation duration:** ~5 minutes for triage and acknowledgment, plus a scheduled maintenance window for the physical fix (powershelf PSUs are hot-swappable; no downtime required for replacement).

## Root cause

Determine feed vs. hardware first — it changes who owns the fix:

1. **Loss of PDU feed** to the PSU's AC input (facilities issue: PDU breaker, upstream panel, or utility transfer event). Indicated by `Input Lost` state and often a preceding `InputVoltageWarning`; the PSU itself is healthy.
2. **PSU hardware failure** (internal fault). Indicated by `Failed` state with the AC feed confirmed live at the receptacle.
3. **Tripped rack branch circuit breaker** for that PSU's circuit. Check whether other equipment on the same branch dropped at the same timestamp. At 5500 W per supply these branches run close to their rating, so a marginal breaker trips here before it trips anywhere else in the rack.

## Remediation steps

### Step 1 — Acknowledge PSU fault in iDRAC (`acknowledge_alert`)

Acknowledge the active alert so escalation paging stops while the physical investigation proceeds. Export the SEL before clearing it — it is the primary evidence for a Dell dispatch:

```bash
racadm lclog export -f psu_incident_$(date +%Y%m%d).xml
racadm clrsel
```

Or via iDRAC UI: Maintenance → System Event Log → Save, then Clear. Do not silence PSU monitoring itself — a second PSU event in this window must page immediately, and it must page every sled owner on the shelf, not just this one.

### Step 2 — Schedule PSU replacement with facilities (`schedule_maintenance`)

1. Identify the faulted slot and state:

   ```bash
   racadm getsensorinfo | grep -iA2 "PS[0-9]"
   ```

   (or iDRAC UI → Power → Power Supplies). Note whether the state is `Input Lost` (feed problem) or `Failed` (hardware).

2. **If Input Lost:** dispatch facilities to check the PDU outlet and rack branch breaker for that feed. Restoring the feed typically clears the fault within seconds with no part replacement.
3. **If Failed with feed confirmed live:** raise a Dell hardware dispatch for a replacement 5500 W supply. Powershelf PSUs are hot-swappable — schedule the swap as a low-risk window; no node drain is needed, but the swap should not proceed while the shelf is over budget and capped at full load. Notify the owners of every sled on the shelf before the window: the swap is safe, but it briefly runs the domain at the same single-fault exposure that opened this incident.

### Step 3 — Confirm remaining PSU load within limits (`verify_redundancy`)

While running degraded, confirm the surviving supplies can carry the connected load:

```bash
racadm getsensorinfo | grep -i "psu\|watt"
```

If draw exceeds the reduced N+1 budget (as in the excerpt above), the rack power manager will already have capped GPU power; you may cap explicitly to a known-safe value instead of relying on the automatic budget:

```bash
nvidia-smi -pl 900    # applies to all GPUs; sets 900 W cap
```

Apply the same cap on every sled in the power domain — capping one node while its neighbor runs uncapped leaves the shelf over budget and rebuilds no margin at all.

After the PSU or feed is restored, verify redundancy is re-established (`racadm getsensorinfo` shows all six PSUs `Present`/`OK` and the Lifecycle Log records redundancy regained), then restore full GPU power:

```bash
nvidia-smi -pl 1100
```

## Escalation

- Feed confirmed live but PSU still shows `Failed` → Dell on-site dispatch for hot-swap replacement. Provide the exported Lifecycle Log and `racadm getsensorinfo` output.
- Repeated `InputVoltageWarning` events across multiple PSUs on the same PDU branch → escalate to facilities/electrical as a shared-infrastructure problem before it takes the branch out and with it half the shelf.
- Second PSU loss on the same shelf while degraded → treat as Sev 1 for every sled in the power domain: begin an orderly drain of all of them immediately to protect against unplanned shutdown.
- Shelf routinely running within a few hundred watts of its N+1 budget at steady state → escalate as a capacity-planning problem rather than an incident; a domain that cannot absorb one PSU loss without capping is under-provisioned for the sleds connected to it.
