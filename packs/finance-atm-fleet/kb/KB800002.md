# Card Reader Transport Fault: Card Capture Error on Diebold Nixdorf ActivEdge Reader

| | |
|---|---|
| **Article ID** | KB800002 |
| **Version** | 4 |
| **Last updated** | 2025-08-22 |
| **Classification** | Break/Fix — Card Reader / Motorized Transport |
| **Default severity** | Critical (Sev 2 — card transactions down, contactless may remain available) |
| **Audience** | ATM field service technicians, branch operations, customer service |
| **Applies to** | Diebold Nixdorf ActivEdge secure card reader, DN Series 200 |

## Summary

The ActivEdge motorized card reader draws a card in, reads the chip, and ejects it via a motor-driven transport. A transport fault occurs when the eject cycle's motor stall or position sensor readings indicate the card did not complete its return path — the reader's fault-safe response is to route the card into the internal capture bin rather than leave it stuck mid-transport where a customer might be injured attempting to remove it. This protects the customer physically but creates an immediate customer-service obligation: a captured card must be tracked (BIN and last four digits are logged for this purpose) and made available for retrieval or returned to the customer per the institution's procedure — it is not resolved simply by clearing the mechanical fault. Card transactions (chip/magstripe) are suspended for the duration of the fault; many DN Series 200 configurations have an independently-wired contactless module that continues operating, allowing tap transactions to proceed even while the motorized reader is offline.

## Affected products

- Diebold Nixdorf ActivEdge secure card reader
- Diebold Nixdorf DN Series 200 ATM fleet

## Error signature

The canonical signatures for this fault are:

- `Card reader transport fault`
- `card reader offline`
- `card capture error`

Card reader device log:

```
CRIT  reader[atm-04]: Card reader transport fault — card reader offline, card capture error, card transactions suspended
CRIT  reader[atm-04]: Card retained in transport — routed to capture bin per fault-safe procedure
```

## Symptoms

- Motor stall detected during the eject cycle, with abnormal motor current draw
- Eject retries (typically up to 3) fail, with the card position sensor continuing to report the card still in transport
- Reader declares itself offline and routes the card to the internal capture bin as a fault-safe action
- EJ (electronic journal) logs a card capture event including the card's BIN and last 4 digits, specifically to support retrieval/return tracking
- If the terminal has an independently-wired contactless module, tap transactions continue to function — the ATM shows PARTIAL_SERVICE rather than fully out of service

## Sample log excerpt

Representative sequence from `atm-04` (Diebold Nixdorf DN Series 200, Westside Branch):

```
16:19:07Z INFO  reader[atm-04]: Card inserted — motorized transport engaged
16:19:09Z WARN  reader[atm-04]: Card reader motor stall detected during eject cycle — retry initiated
16:19:11Z WARN  reader[atm-04]: Eject retry 1/3 — motor current draw abnormal
16:19:14Z WARN  reader[atm-04]: Eject retry 2/3 — card position sensor reports card still in transport
16:19:18Z CRIT  reader[atm-04]: Card reader transport fault — card reader offline, card capture error, card transactions suspended
16:19:18Z CRIT  reader[atm-04]: Card retained in transport — routed to capture bin per fault-safe procedure
16:19:19Z INFO  reader[atm-04]: Captured card bin count — 1 (session start: 0)
16:19:22Z INFO  ndc[atm-04]: Contactless reader — independent module, no fault, tap transactions available
```

The two failed eject retries with escalating diagnostic detail (motor current, then position sensor) before the fault-safe capture is the expected fault-tolerant sequence — a reader that captures on the very first stall without attempting retries would itself be worth flagging as a possible retry-logic defect.

## Impact

- **Workload impact:** Chip and magstripe card transactions fail entirely until resolved. Contactless (tap) transactions may continue if the terminal has an independently-wired contactless module — this ATM shows PARTIAL_SERVICE rather than a full outage.
- **Service risk:** Medium. Beyond the mechanical fault, a card captured by a faulted reader must be tracked and returned to the customer. Failure to do so promptly is a customer-service issue and, depending on jurisdiction, a potential regulatory complaint (unreturned card retention) — treat captured-card handling as equally urgent to the mechanical remediation, not a lower-priority follow-up.
- **Estimated remediation duration:** ~5 minutes for a remote reset attempt. Field dispatch for physical clear and card retrieval/return handling is typically same-day.

## Root cause

In observed order of frequency:

1. **Transport motor wear** — degraded motor performance under normal cyclic load eventually fails to complete an eject stroke cleanly, especially in high-transaction-volume locations.
2. **Card physically damaged or foreign object in reader** — a bent, cracked, or otherwise non-standard card can stall the transport mechanically even with a healthy motor.
3. **Position sensor contamination or misalignment** — dust or wear on the card-position sensor produces a false "card still in transport" reading even when the card has physically cleared, causing an unnecessary capture.
4. **Firmware/control board fault** — less common; suspected when the fault recurs across multiple card types and physical inspection finds no mechanical obstruction, pointing at the reader's control electronics rather than the transport itself.

## Remediation steps

### Step 1 — Issue remote card reader reset command (`remote_reader_reset`)

Attempt before dispatch:

```
ATM monitoring platform: select ATM-04 → Card Reader → Reset
Confirm command acknowledged and reader reports post-reset status
```

A reset will not retrieve a card already routed to the capture bin — captured cards require physical retrieval regardless of reset outcome, which is why Step 2 proceeds in parallel rather than after.

### Step 2 — Verify captured-card bin status and log the retained card (`verify_captured_card_handling`)

```
Confirm capture bin count and cross-reference against the EJ-logged BIN/last-4 for the captured card
Flag the captured card for retrieval in the branch's card-retention tracking system
Initiate customer notification per institution procedure if the cardholder can be identified from the transaction
```

This step runs regardless of whether Step 1's reset succeeds — the physical card is captured either way and must be tracked.

### Step 3 — Enable contactless-only mode if configured (`enable_contactless_fallback`)

```
Confirm terminal configuration supports independent contactless operation (verify via ndc[atm-04] contactless module status)
If supported and reader remains offline after Step 1, confirm PARTIAL_SERVICE status is accurately reflected to keep contactless transactions available
Update customer-facing signage/screen messaging to indicate card transactions unavailable, tap available
```

### Step 4 — Dispatch field engineer to physically clear reader and retrieve captured cards (`dispatch_field_engineer_reader`)

```
Dispatch with explicit task: physical transport inspection/clear AND captured-card retrieval
Field engineer to log retrieved card details against the EJ capture record and route to branch for customer return per procedure
Confirm reader passes self-test before returning ATM to full card-transaction service
```

## Escalation

- Reader captures a card without completing the eject-retry sequence logged in this event (i.e., captures on first stall) → escalate to field service as a possible retry-logic or firmware defect, separate from routine mechanical wear.
- Captured card cannot be matched to a transaction record, or customer identity cannot be established → escalate to branch operations / compliance for the institution's unclaimed-card procedure.
- Reader fault recurs on the same unit within 14 days → escalate to Diebold Nixdorf field service for reader replacement rather than repeat reset/clear cycles; recurring transport faults this close together indicate a hardware issue reset alone won't fix.
