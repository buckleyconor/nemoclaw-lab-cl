# Battery Depleted: Large-Volume Infusion Pump 8100 Battery End-of-Life

| | |
|---|---|
| **Article ID** | KB700001 |
| **Version** | 4 |
| **Last updated** | 2025-12-03 |
| **Classification** | Preventive Maintenance — Power System |
| **Audience** | Clinical engineering / biomedical technicians, nursing staff |
| **Applies to** | PC Unit 8015 with Pump Module 8100, NiMH battery pack |
| **Note** | This is an equipment maintenance article. It does not address medication dosing or clinical infusion decisions — those remain the clinical team's responsibility. |

## Summary

The infusion pump PC Unit runs on a rechargeable NiMH (nickel metal hydride) battery pack that supplies power during transport or any period the pump is disconnected from AC. Battery run time is a function of infusion rate and remaining charge, and the pump surfaces a low-battery warning well ahead of depletion. As the battery ages through repeated charge cycles, its usable runtime at full charge degrades — a pack rated for roughly 300 typical service-life cycles (500 maximum) can drop well below its original runtime long before it fails outright, meaning a pump can pass its daily power-on self-test yet still run out of battery mid-transport. This article covers the routine end-of-life battery replacement workflow; it is distinct from a battery alarm *failing to fire*, which is a safety-critical defect covered under a separate manufacturer field action, not this article's remediation path.

## Affected products

- Infusion pump PC Unit, Models 8015
- Pump Module 8100
- NiMH rechargeable battery pack (all infusion pump system configurations using this pack)

## Error signature

The canonical signatures for this fault are:

- `Battery depleted`
- `battery run time low`
- `battery end of life`

Infusion pump PC unit device log:

```
WARN  infusionpump[infusion-pump-01]: Battery run time low — estimated 8 minutes remaining at current infusion rate
CRIT  infusionpump[infusion-pump-01]: Battery depleted — pump switched to AC power required, alarm silenced by AC connection
```

## Symptoms

- Estimated runtime at a given charge percentage is noticeably shorter than the pump's original full-charge runtime for the same infusion rate
- Low-battery audible/visual alarm fires during routine transport that previously completed without incident
- Device log shows charge-cycle count approaching or exceeding the pack's typical service-life rating (300 cycles typical, 500 maximum)
- Battery health/capacity flag in the device log below 60% of rated runtime at a reported full charge
- Pump requires AC reconnection sooner than expected after being taken off charge

## Sample log excerpt

Representative sequence from `infusion-pump-01` during a routine intra-facility patient transport:

```
07:02:14Z INFO  infusionpump[infusion-pump-01]: Battery status — 34% charge, NiMH pack, est. runtime 22 min
07:02:14Z INFO  infusionpump[infusion-pump-01]: Patient transport in progress — 3W to Radiology, AC disconnected 07:01:50
07:12:07Z WARN  infusionpump[infusion-pump-01]: Battery run time low — estimated 8 minutes remaining at current infusion rate
07:16:52Z CRIT  infusionpump[infusion-pump-01]: Battery depleted — pump switched to AC power required, alarm silenced by AC connection
07:16:53Z INFO  infusionpump[infusion-pump-01]: Battery charge cycle count — 412 (rated service life: 300 cycles typical, 500 max)
07:16:53Z WARN  infusionpump[infusion-pump-01]: Battery health flag — capacity degraded below 60% of rated runtime at full charge
```

The transport in this example completed without an interruption to delivery because AC was available on arrival — the fault this article addresses is the degraded capacity behind the alarm, not this specific transport's outcome. A longer transport, an elevator delay, or a delayed AC reconnection on the next occurrence would not have the same safe margin.

## Impact

- **Workload impact:** Swapping to a spare pump takes roughly 2 minutes and does not interrupt the active infusion. No impact to dosing accuracy during the swap when performed per Step 1.
- **Service risk:** Medium. A pump with a degraded battery that is dispatched for transport without AC access risks the infusion stopping mid-transport if the low-battery alarm window is shorter than the transport duration. Do not send an affected pump on transport duty until the battery is replaced or independently verified to meet rated runtime.
- **Estimated remediation duration:** ~10 minutes to swap the active infusion to a spare pump and tag the affected unit out of service. Battery replacement and post-replacement runtime verification is a biomedical workshop task performed outside the clinical remediation window.

## Root cause

1. **Normal battery aging past typical service-life cycle count** — as seen in this event (412 cycles logged against a 300-cycle typical rating), NiMH packs lose usable capacity progressively with charge cycles; this is expected wear, not a device malfunction.
2. **Repeated partial-charge cycles** — pumps frequently placed on and taken off charger before reaching full charge can accelerate apparent capacity loss versus full charge/discharge cycling.
3. **Pack age independent of cycle count** — NiMH chemistry self-discharges and degrades with calendar age even under light use; a pump used infrequently can still need replacement on a time-based PM schedule.
4. **Ambient temperature extremes during storage or charging** — sustained heat exposure (e.g., pump left charging near a heat source) accelerates capacity fade beyond the cycle-count-driven baseline.

## Remediation steps

### Step 1 — Swap in a spare pump with a healthy battery (`swap_spare_pump`)

Preserve continuity of the active infusion:

```
Verify spare pump battery status — confirm >80% charge and no low-battery/health flags in its device log
Transfer infusion parameters (rate, VTBI, drug library entry) to the spare pump per standard channel transfer procedure
Confirm new pump running before disconnecting the affected pump
```

### Step 2 — Tag affected pump out-of-service in CMMS (`tag_out_of_service`)

```
Log the battery health flag and cycle count from the device log into the CMMS work order
Attach an out-of-service tag physically on the pump — do not rely on CMMS status alone to keep it out of clinical circulation
Route to biomedical engineering queue for battery replacement
```

### Step 3 — Replace battery pack per manufacturer-authorized service procedure (`replace_battery_pack`)

Performed by biomedical engineering, not clinical staff:

```
Follow the PC Unit Technical Service Manual battery replacement procedure for the PC Unit 8015
Use only manufacturer-authorized replacement NiMH battery packs — third-party or non-authorized packs are not validated against the pump's runtime estimation firmware
Record replacement date and reset cycle-count tracking in the CMMS asset record
```

### Step 4 — Verify full-charge runtime before returning to service (`verify_runtime_pm`)

```
Charge new battery to 100% per device indicator
Run a controlled discharge test at a representative infusion rate (e.g. 125 mL/hr) and confirm runtime meets or exceeds the manufacturer's rated spec
Document verified runtime in the PM record before releasing the pump back to clinical inventory
```

Do not return the pump to circulation, especially for transport duty, without this verification — a replacement battery that itself underperforms rated spec (a defective unit, wrong part) would otherwise go undetected until the next low-battery event.

## Escalation

- Newly replaced battery fails to meet rated runtime in Step 4 verification → do not release to service; escalate to the pump manufacturer's technical support for the replacement part, possible defective unit.
- Low-battery alarm fails to fire at all despite confirmed low charge (found during any testing) → stop use immediately and escalate as a potential safety-critical alarm defect, separate from this routine end-of-life article — report per your facility's medical device adverse event and recall-monitoring procedure.
- Multiple pumps in the same clinical area showing accelerated cycle counts within a short period → escalate to clinical engineering leadership; may indicate a charging station fault or a workflow issue causing excessive partial-charge cycling fleet-wide.
