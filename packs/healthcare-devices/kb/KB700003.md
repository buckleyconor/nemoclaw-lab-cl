# Coldhead Failure: MRI Cryocooler Stop and Rising Helium Pressure on 1.5T/3T MRI Scanners

| | |
|---|---|
| **Article ID** | KB700003 |
| **Version** | 3 |
| **Last updated** | 2026-03-01 |
| **Classification** | Urgent — Cryogenic System / Magnet Support Equipment |
| **Default severity** | Critical (Sev 1 — quench risk if unresolved, scanner locked out of clinical use) |
| **Audience** | Clinical/imaging engineering, MRI physicist, scanner field service liaison |
| **Applies to** | 1.5T/3T wide-bore MRI scanners with two-stage Gifford-McMahon coldhead cryocooler |

## Summary

The MRI superconducting magnet is kept below its critical temperature by liquid helium, continuously re-condensed by a two-stage coldhead cryocooler. The coldhead's compressor cycles helium gas through the cold head to keep the second stage near 4.2K, which recondenses helium gas boiled off by ordinary heat leak back into liquid. When the coldhead fails — compressor stop, valve fault, or worn drive motor — recondensation halts. Heat load then boils off helium continuously with nothing recondensing it, and the pressurized helium in the containment vessel heats and its pressure climbs. This is a slow-building emergency, not an instantaneous one: the scanner has margin before its pressure relief threshold, but that margin shrinks continuously while the coldhead is down. If pressure is not brought back under control, the magnet can quench — an abrupt, uncontrolled loss of superconductivity that vents helium and takes the scanner offline for days to weeks for helium refill and a magnet ramp back to field. The scanner locks itself out of clinical scanning the moment the fault is detected, independent of any remediation outcome.

## Affected products

- Wide-bore MRI scanners (1.5T and 3T configurations with two-stage GM cryocooler)
- Compressor and coldhead assembly (OEM cryogenics components supplied under the scanner manufacturer's service branding)

## Error signature

The canonical signatures for this fault are:

- `Coldhead failure`
- `helium pressure rising`
- `cryocooler service required`

Cryogenics monitoring system:

```
WARN  cryo[mri-01]: Compressor cycling irregular — coldhead second-stage temperature trending above nominal
CRIT  cryo[mri-01]: Coldhead failure — compressor stopped, helium pressure rising, cryocooler service required
```

## Symptoms

- Coldhead cycling frequency drops from its nominal rate (commonly ~1-1.5 Hz) well before a full stop — an early warning window if the trend is caught
- Second-stage temperature climbs above its nominal ~4.2K setpoint, first gradually then rapidly once recondensation effectively stops
- Helium containment pressure trends upward steadily with no active cooling to offset it
- Compressor motor current draw may show an abnormal pattern (elevated or erratic) in the period leading up to a full stop, consistent with bearing wear or a valve fault
- Scanner console locks out clinical scanning automatically once the cryogenics fault is flagged
- Audible change in compressor sound (knocking, irregular cycling) is sometimes reported by MRI technologists before the fault registers on monitoring — worth soliciting during triage

## Sample log excerpt

Representative sequence from `mri-01` (1.5T Wide-Bore MRI):

```
02:00:00Z INFO  cryo[mri-01]: Coldhead cycling nominal — 1.2 Hz, second-stage temp 4.2K, helium pressure 1.8 bar
05:14:22Z WARN  cryo[mri-01]: Compressor cycling irregular — coldhead second-stage temperature trending above nominal
05:14:22Z WARN  cryo[mri-01]: Second-stage temp 6.8K (nominal 4.2K), cycling frequency dropped to 0.6 Hz
05:31:03Z WARN  cryo[mri-01]: Compressor motor current draw elevated — possible bearing wear or valve fault
05:38:19Z CRIT  cryo[mri-01]: Coldhead failure — compressor stopped, helium pressure rising, cryocooler service required
05:38:19Z CRIT  cryo[mri-01]: Second-stage temp 11.4K and climbing — recondensation halted
06:10:33Z WARN  cryo[mri-01]: Helium pressure 3.0 bar — pressure trend continuing, no active cooling
06:10:33Z WARN  cryo[mri-01]: Quench relief valve setpoint reference — 5.5 bar, current margin 2.5 bar
```

The cycling-frequency drop (1.2 Hz to 0.6 Hz) roughly 24 minutes before the full stop is the earliest actionable signal in this sequence — flagging service on that trend, rather than waiting for the full stop, is the difference between a scheduled coldhead service and an urgent quench-risk event.

## Impact

- **Workload impact:** If the compressor does not restart successfully, the scanner must be taken out of clinical service immediately — all exams scheduled on this unit need rescheduling or diversion to another scanner until resolved.
- **Service risk:** High. Sustained coldhead failure allows continuous helium boil-off with nothing recondensing it. If pressure climbs past the relief threshold without the coldhead being restored, the magnet quenches — venting helium abruptly and taking the scanner offline for days to weeks for a full helium refill and magnet field ramp. This is the single most expensive and disruptive failure mode this equipment fleet has.
- **Estimated remediation duration:** ~15 minutes to attempt a restart and assess whether it holds. If unsuccessful, vendor service engagement and any required helium top-off can take from hours to several days depending on parts and helium logistics.

## Root cause

1. **Compressor motor or bearing wear** — as suggested by the elevated/erratic current draw preceding the stop in this event; mechanical wear in the compressor drive is the most common coldhead failure mode over years of service.
2. **Helium gas line valve fault** — a stuck or degraded rotary valve in the coldhead disrupts the gas cycling that drives the cooling process, producing the same cycling-frequency and temperature signature without a compressor motor fault.
3. **Water cooling loop fault (for water-cooled compressor variants)** — inadequate compressor cooling water flow or temperature causes the compressor to trip on its own thermal protection, distinguishable by checking facility chilled water supply alongside the cryo log.
4. **Helium contamination in the closed cryogenic loop** — accumulated contaminants degrade coldhead efficiency gradually over a longer period than the abrupt-stop pattern here; more often presents as a slow efficiency decline than a sudden failure.

## Remediation steps

### Step 1 — Attempt compressor restart (`restart_compressor`)

Time-critical — attempt promptly since every minute the coldhead is down allows further helium boil-off:

```
Follow the scanner manufacturer's cryogenics restart procedure from the scanner's technical service console
Confirm compressor water cooling supply (if water-cooled variant) is flowing and within spec before restart
Observe compressor start current and initial cycling behavior closely
```

### Step 2 — Verify coldhead second-stage temperature returns to nominal (`verify_coldhead_cycling`)

```
Monitor second-stage temperature for return toward 4.2K nominal over the following 15-30 minutes
Confirm cycling frequency returns to nominal rate (~1-1.5 Hz)
A restart that holds should show a clear downward temperature trend within this window — a restart that cycles briefly then faults again indicates the underlying fault (Step 1's root cause) was not resolved by power-cycling alone
```

### Step 3 — Open a manufacturer service case if restart does not hold (`open_vendor_service_case`)

```
If temperature does not trend down within 30 minutes, or the compressor faults again after restart, open a manufacturer service case immediately — do not attempt repeated restart cycles, which can mask the underlying mechanical fault while helium continues to boil off
Provide the cycling-frequency and temperature trend log from this event to accelerate field service diagnosis
Request priority dispatch given active quench-risk status
```

### Step 4 — Monitor helium level and pressure trend continuously (`monitor_helium_trend`)

```
Continue active monitoring of helium pressure and level until the coldhead is confirmed restored and cycling nominally
Compare current pressure margin against the magnet's quench relief valve setpoint (this system: 5.5 bar) at each check
Escalate immediately per Escalation section below if margin narrows faster than the trend in this event, or reaches within 1 bar of the relief setpoint
```

## Escalation

- Restart does not hold, or temperature/pressure trend does not reverse within 30 minutes of any restart attempt → escalate immediately to the scanner manufacturer's field service as urgent/quench-risk priority — do not wait for a routine service window.
- Helium pressure reaches within 1 bar of the quench relief valve setpoint at any point → escalate immediately to facilities/imaging engineering leadership and notify the scanner manufacturer of imminent quench risk; prepare the room per facility quench-venting emergency procedure regardless of whether a quench ultimately occurs.
- Coldhead failure recurs within 90 days of a prior service visit on the same unit → escalate as a possible incomplete repair or recurring root cause (e.g., persistent water cooling issue) rather than routine wear, and request a manufacturer root-cause analysis rather than a repeat like-for-like part swap.
