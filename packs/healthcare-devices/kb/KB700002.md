# Telemetry Disconnect: No Data from Bed on Philips IntelliVue / PIC iX Central Station

|---|---|

| **Version** | 5 |
| **Last updated** | 2026-02-17 |
| **Classification** | Break/Fix — Network / Telemetry Connectivity |
| **Default severity** | Critical (Sev 1 — central monitoring visibility lost, bedside monitoring unaffected) |
| **Audience** | Clinical engineering, hospital IT/network team, telemetry technicians |
| **Applies to** | Philips IntelliVue MX450/MX800 monitors, Patient Information Center iX (PIC iX) central station, hospital WLAN infrastructure |
| **Note** | This is a connectivity/equipment article. It does not address clinical response to a patient event — refer to unit escalation policy for coverage during a data gap. |

## Summary

Philips IntelliVue monitors stream continuous vitals to the PIC iX central station over the hospital's wireless network. When the monitor's wireless association degrades or fails — most commonly during a roam between access points — the data stream to the central station interrupts, and PIC iX raises a "No Data from Bed" condition once the gap exceeds its configured threshold. Critically, this is a *central-station visibility* fault, not a monitor malfunction: the bedside monitor's own display, local alarms, and vitals acquisition (ECG, SpO2, NIBP) continue functioning normally throughout — the patient is not left unmonitored at the bedside, but staff relying on the central station or remote telemetry view lose visibility to this bed until the connection is restored. Any response to this alert should confirm bedside monitoring is intact (it almost always is) while working the network fix in parallel, not treat it as a patient-monitoring gap requiring bedside intervention.

## Affected products

- Philips IntelliVue MX450, MX800 monitors (wireless telemetry configurations)
- Patient Information Center iX (PIC iX) central station, Release B.00 and later
- Hospital WLAN access point infrastructure serving telemetry units

## Error signature

The canonical signatures for this fault are:

- `Telemetry disconnect`
- `No data from bed`
- `central station connection lost`

Central station / monitor device log:

```
CRIT  intellivue[monitor-02]: Network connection to PIC iX lost — INOP: Network
CRIT  pic-ix[central]: No Data from Bed 12 — central station lost connection to monitor-02, last update 47s ago
```

## Symptoms

- WLAN signal quality (RSSI) degrades and retry rate climbs above baseline (commonly <5%) before the disconnect — an early warning window if monitored proactively
- A roam attempt to a neighboring access point fails (authentication timeout or association failure)
- PIC iX displays a "No Data" banner on the affected bed's tile, typically after ~30-60 seconds of missing updates
- The monitor itself continues showing an `INOP: Network` technical alarm locally, while its ECG/SpO2/NIBP acquisition and bedside display/alarms remain active and unaffected
- Telemetry technicians or charge nurses are notified per unit escalation policy when a bed shows sustained "No Data"

## Sample log excerpt

Representative sequence from `monitor-02` (IntelliVue MX450, Telemetry Bed 12):

```
14:20:00Z INFO  wlan[monitor-02]: Associated AP: AP-3W-04, RSSI -58 dBm, nominal
14:41:12Z WARN  wlan[monitor-02]: WLAN signal quality degraded — associated AP roam failure, retry count elevated
14:41:12Z WARN  wlan[monitor-02]: RSSI -79 dBm, retry count 34% (baseline <5%)
14:41:38Z WARN  wlan[monitor-02]: Roam attempt to AP-3W-05 failed — authentication timeout
14:42:19Z CRIT  intellivue[monitor-02]: Network connection to PIC iX lost — INOP: Network
14:42:19Z CRIT  pic-ix[central]: No Data from Bed 12 — central station lost connection to monitor-02, last update 47s ago
14:42:19Z INFO  intellivue[monitor-02]: Bedside monitoring unaffected — local display and alarms active, ECG/SpO2/NIBP continuing
```

The RSSI drop from -58 dBm to -79 dBm over roughly 20 minutes, followed by a failed roam attempt, is the actionable early pattern — it points at coverage/roaming rather than a monitor hardware fault.

## Impact

- **Workload impact:** Central station and any remote telemetry viewing lose visibility to this bed until reconnected. Bedside alarms and the local monitor display continue functioning normally throughout — there is no gap in the patient's actual physiological monitoring at the bedside.
- **Service risk:** High. Central monitoring is a primary safety net specifically for telemetry patients not under continuous 1:1 bedside observation; a prolonged "No Data" condition means remote staff cannot see vitals or arrhythmia alerts for this patient until resolved.
- **Estimated remediation duration:** ~5 minutes for a re-association fix. A persistent access-point or coverage issue affecting multiple beds requires hospital IT network team involvement and is outside the immediate remediation window.

## Root cause

1. **AP roam failure during handoff** — as seen in this event, the monitor attempts to roam to a neighboring access point (patient movement, unit layout) and the roam fails authentication or association, dropping the connection rather than handing off cleanly.
2. **RF coverage gap or interference** — a dead zone or new source of 2.4/5 GHz interference (new equipment, construction, microwave-adjacent placement) degrading signal below usable threshold in a specific location.
3. **Access point overload or fault** — the associated AP itself is saturated with clients or has a firmware/hardware fault causing it to drop associations under load.
4. **Monitor wireless radio fault** — less common; isolated to one specific monitor experiencing disconnects across multiple locations/APs where other monitors nearby stay connected, which distinguishes a device-level fault from a network/coverage fault.

## Remediation steps

### Step 1 — Verify wireless AP health and coverage at the bed location (`verify_ap_health`)

```
Check AP-3W-04 and AP-3W-05 status in the WLAN controller dashboard — client count, error rate, uptime
Confirm no active maintenance, firmware update, or known outage affecting this coverage area
Compare RSSI reported by other monitors currently associated to the same APs — an isolated report suggests device-level, a cluster suggests AP/coverage
```

### Step 2 — Re-associate or restart the monitor's wireless network interface (`reassociate_network`)

```
On the monitor: Main Setup → Network → re-initiate wireless association
If re-association does not resolve within 60 seconds, power-cycle the monitor's network module (or full monitor restart per device procedure)
Confirm monitor re-associates to an AP with acceptable RSSI (better than -70 dBm)
```

### Step 3 — Confirm live waveform and vitals restored at the central station (`confirm_waveform_restored`)

```
At PIC iX: confirm Bed 12 tile shows live, updating waveform and current vitals
Confirm "No Data" banner cleared and timestamp is current
Verify with bedside/charge nurse that no alarms were missed during the gap per unit escalation policy
```

### Step 4 — Escalate to hospital IT/network team if disconnect recurs (`escalate_network_team`)

```
If the same bed/location disconnects again within the same shift, or multiple beds in the same area are affected, open a ticket with hospital IT network team
Provide AP association history and RSSI trend from Step 1 to accelerate root-cause diagnosis
```

## Escalation

- Disconnect recurs at the same physical location after AP verification finds nothing abnormal → escalate to IT network team for a site survey; may indicate a genuine RF coverage gap requiring AP placement changes.
- Multiple beds/monitors in the same unit lose central-station connectivity simultaneously → treat as a unit-wide network event, not a per-monitor fault; escalate immediately to IT network team and notify charge nurse for manual monitoring coverage per unit policy.
- Monitor continues disconnecting across multiple locations/APs while other monitors at the same locations stay connected → escalate to Philips biomedical service for wireless radio module diagnosis; likely device-level fault, not network.
